This guide is for the apartment with no balcony: one or two windows, a radiator, and no outdoor space you are allowed to use. The constraint is light, and a root crop feels it more than a leaf crop. Beets fit anyway, because every beet is a leaf crop first and a root crop second.

Chioggia, the beet in the Happy Seeds kit, is the worked example: a cool-season variety with pink and white rings inside, 50 to 60 days to a mature root outdoors. The plan is a pot 8 to 10 inches deep, a bright window or a lamp, one careful round of thinning, and scissors for the greens.

## Can you grow beets indoors from seed?

Yes, beets grow indoors from seed, and the greens are the dependable part of the harvest. University of Maryland Extension's guide to [growing beets in a home garden](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-beets-home-garden) says beets tolerate partial shade and grow well with 4 to 6 hours of direct light a day. A south or west window clears that bar for most of the year.

Roots are the part that depends on your light. A plant with a couple of hours of sun makes leaves and a thin root. One in a full south window, or under a lamp, can make a root the size of a golf ball. The series post on [five vegetables for a windowsill](/blog/windowsill-vegetables-from-seed/) lists beet greens for the same reason.

## What pot do beets need indoors?

Beets need a container 8 to 10 inches deep with a drainage hole, filled with ordinary potting mix rather than garden soil. Colorado State University Extension's guide to [vegetable gardening in containers](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/vegetable-gardening-in-containers) lists beets at 8 inches deep, thinned to 3 inches apart. The kit's container note for Chioggia is 10 inches deep, and the extra depth helps the root if your light is good.

| Goal | Container | Depth | Final spacing | First harvest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greens only, cut and come again | Window box or wide tray | 8 inches | 2 inches | 21 to 40 days |
| Greens plus small roots | Window box or wide pot | 8 to 10 inches | 3 inches | Greens at a month, roots at 70 to 90 days |

A window box 24 inches long and 10 inches deep holds about eight plants at 3 inches apart. Put a saucer under it, and a tray under that if you rent. The series post on [where to start seeds in a small apartment](/blog/starting-seeds-in-a-small-apartment/) has the shelf-and-tray layout.

## How do you sow beet seeds indoors?

Sow beet seeds a half inch deep, about an inch apart, in damp potting mix, in a room at 60 to 70 degrees F. Illinois Extension's [beet page](https://extension.illinois.edu/gardening/beet) gives that depth and spacing. Oregon State University's [soil temperature table](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/soil-temperature-conditions-vegetable-seed-germination) puts emergence at about 10 days near 60 degrees F and 6 days near 70 degrees F.

1. Soak the seeds in water for 6 to 12 hours; Maryland Extension's [indoor greens guide](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-microgreens-and-baby-greens-indoors) says it speeds beet germination.
2. Fill the box to an inch below the rim with potting mix and water until evenly damp.
3. Press seeds in an inch apart and cover with a half inch of mix.
4. Keep the surface damp and the box off the radiator; the series post on [radiators and indoor seedlings](/blog/radiator-heat-and-indoor-seedlings/) explains why.
5. Move the box into your brightest light the day the first loops of stem appear.

The general rules for seed depth are in [how deep to plant seeds](/blog/how-deep-to-plant-seeds/).

## Why does one beet seed come up as a clump, and how do you thin it?

One beet seed comes up as a clump because it is a dried fruit holding several seeds, and you thin the clump to one plant with scissors. Maryland Extension calls it a seed ball with several embryos and says to thin when the seedlings are about 2 inches tall. Illinois Extension says thinning is almost always necessary unless the seed was singulated, so plan on it with ordinary beet seed.

Cut the extra seedlings at soil level instead of pulling them, because the tangled roots come up together. Leave the strongest seedling every 2 inches for greens or every 3 inches for roots. Illinois Extension notes that thinnings taken at 3 inches tall can be cooked like spinach.

## How much light do indoor beets need?

Indoor beets need a south or west window with several hours of direct sun, or a lamp running about 16 hours a day above the leaves. Maryland Extension's indoor greens guide gives 16 hours of total light for baby greens, with the lamp 6 to 12 inches above the foliage.

In winter, an east or north window tends to grow thin, pale greens and little or no root. The series posts on [which window direction is best](/blog/best-window-direction-for-growing-vegetables/) and [grow lights for apartment seedlings](/blog/grow-lights-for-apartment-seedlings/) cover judging your exposure and a basic lamp. Stretched seedlings are a light problem; the fix is in [leggy seedlings indoors](/blog/leggy-seedlings-indoors/).

## When do you harvest beet greens, and when do you get a root?

Cut beet greens from about a month after sowing, and pull roots at 1 to 2 inches across; indoors, expect that to take roughly 70 to 90 days. Maryland Extension's indoor greens guide puts baby greens at 4 to 6 inches tall and 21 to 40 days, regrowing once or twice after a cut. Iowa State University Extension's [beet guide](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-beets-home-garden) says up to a third of the foliage can be taken without harming the root, snipped at 6 to 8 inches long.

| Stage | Size | Indoor timing | How to take it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thinnings | 2 to 3 inches tall | Week 2 to 3 | Cut at soil level, eat whole |
| Baby greens | 4 to 6 inches | Day 21 to 40 | Cut outer leaves, leave the center |
| Mature greens | 6 to 8 inches | Day 40 on | Up to a third of the leaves at a time |
| Roots | 1 to 2 inches across | Roughly day 70 to 90, strong light only | Pull the whole plant, greens attached |

Take outer leaves and leave the center, because the remaining leaves feed the root. Illinois Extension says roots past 3 inches turn tough, so pull an indoor beet small. If the root never swells, the plant was short on light, and you still got two months of greens.

## How do you water and feed indoor beets?

Water when the top inch of mix is dry, and feed a half-strength liquid vegetable fertilizer every two to three weeks after the first month. Empty the saucer after watering. Keep the room on the cool side of normal. Illinois Extension says beet seedlings establish most easily in cool, moist conditions, and Colorado State Extension groups beets with the cool-season crops.

## What if you get outdoor space later?

If you later get outdoor space, sow Chioggia 2 to 4 weeks before the last spring frost, and again 8 to 10 weeks before the first fall frost. Confirm those dates with your county extension office; the [state planting calendars](/planting/) give the frost window. Check your [lease and building rules](/blog/balcony-garden-lease-and-building-rules/) and the balcony's [weight limit](/blog/balcony-weight-limits-for-containers/) before setting out a large wet container.
