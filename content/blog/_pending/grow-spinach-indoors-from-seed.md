This guide is for the apartment with no balcony: a window or two, a radiator under one of them, and no outdoor space you are allowed to use. The constraint is light, because an indoor pot gets a fraction of what a garden bed gets. Spinach has a second constraint, bolting, and the apartment turns out to help with that one.

Bloomsdale, the spinach in the Happy Seeds kit, is the worked example: a crinkled, dark-leaved savoy type suited to cool conditions and cut-and-come-again harvests. The plan is a wide shallow box, a cool bright window, a lamp on a short timer in winter, and scissors.

## Can you grow spinach indoors from seed?

Yes, spinach grows indoors from seed, and it suits an apartment better than most vegetables because it wants the cool air and short days a window gives in winter. University of Vermont Extension's guide to [growing edibles indoors](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/mastergardener/news/growing-edibles-indoors) puts leafy greens in a shallow container on a south windowsill, with a lamp for better results.

Expect a cutting crop, not mature bunches. The series post on [five vegetables for a windowsill](/blog/windowsill-vegetables-from-seed/) ranks spinach among the easiest for the same reason.

## Why does spinach bolt, and why does an apartment help?

Spinach bolts because of long days first and warm air second, and an apartment lets you control both. University of Delaware Extension explains that [bolting in spinach happens as days get longer](https://sites.udel.edu/weeklycropupdate/?p=22287), and that high temperatures speed it up. Penn State Extension's guide to [spinach as a cool-season vegetable](https://extension.psu.edu/growing-spinach-a-cool-season-vegetable) adds that it bolts in June even when the weather is cool.

Indoors, the day length is whatever your timer says, and the room is whatever your thermostat says. Penn State says heat and drought stress push spinach to bolt; indoors that means a warm room or a box left to dry out. Keep the lamp on 10 to 12 hours and the pot cool and damp, and the main outdoor trigger never arrives.

Variety matters less than those two controls. New Hampshire Extension says [Bloomsdale Long Standing](https://extension.unh.edu/resource/how-grow-bloomsdale-long-standing-spinach-spinacia-oleracea) is slower to bolt than other spinach. The kit's packet is plain Bloomsdale, a savoy type, so count on the cool room and the timer first.

## How much light does indoor spinach need?

Indoor spinach needs a south or west window with direct sun for several hours, or a lamp running 10 to 12 hours a day. Vermont Extension's guide to [growing greens in winter](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/news/growing-greens-winter) gives that figure and a spot directly in front of a south or west window. Its indoor edibles guide puts the lamp 2 to 4 inches above the tops of the plants.

Do not run the lamp longer to speed things up, because spinach reads a 16-hour day as early summer. Pale, stretched seedlings need a brighter window or a closer lamp, not more hours. The series posts on [which window direction is best](/blog/best-window-direction-for-growing-vegetables/) and [grow lights for apartment seedlings](/blog/grow-lights-for-apartment-seedlings/) cover judging your exposure and a basic lamp setup.

## What pot and potting mix does spinach need indoors?

Spinach needs a container 6 to 8 inches deep with a drainage hole, filled with ordinary potting mix, not garden soil. Vermont Extension allows a shallower container for greens cut very young; for spinach, stay at 6 inches or more. The [UC Marin Master Gardeners spinach sheet](https://ucanr.edu/site/uc-marin-master-gardeners/document/spinach) asks for a pot at least 8 inches deep for full plants.

A wide window box holds more plants per window than a deep round pot, as long as it still gives 6 to 8 inches of depth. Put a saucer under every container, and a tray under it if you rent; the series post on [where to start seeds in a small apartment](/blog/starting-seeds-in-a-small-apartment/) has the layout.

## How do you sow spinach seeds indoors?

Sow spinach seeds a half inch deep in damp, cool potting mix, about an inch apart, and keep the box in a cool room until they sprout. Penn State and New Hampshire Extension both give that depth and want cool soil, and New Hampshire puts sprouting at 7 to 14 days. Keep the box off the radiator and any heat mat.

1. Fill the box to an inch below the rim with potting mix and water until evenly damp.
2. Scatter seeds about an inch apart, or set them in rows 4 inches apart, and cover with a half inch of mix.
3. Keep the surface damp and the box in a cool, bright spot.
4. Move the box into full light the day sprouts appear.
5. Thin at 1 to 2 inches tall, to 2 to 3 inches apart for baby leaves or 4 to 6 inches for full plants, and eat the thinnings.

## What temperature does spinach want indoors?

Spinach wants a cool room, 60 to 70 degrees F, and UC Marin says it grows fastest at 60 to 65 degrees F. Vermont Extension gives that range for indoor greens and warns against hot, drying drafts from radiators and heating vents.

The spot to avoid is the sill directly above a steam radiator, where afternoon air runs far warmer than the room. A shelf to the side of the window suits spinach better; the series post on [radiators and indoor seedlings](/blog/radiator-heat-and-indoor-seedlings/) explains how to find that cool bright spot.

## How do you water and harvest indoor spinach?

Water when the top half inch of mix is dry, and harvest by cutting outer leaves at 3 to 4 inches from about a month after sowing. Penn State warns that spinach bolts when the pot dries out. A shallow box dries faster than a deep pot, so check it every day or two with a finger and empty the saucer after watering.

Feeding is light: a half-strength liquid vegetable fertilizer every two to three weeks after the first month is plenty. Cutting outer leaves and leaving the center is Vermont Extension's cut-and-come-again method, and it gives several harvests from one sowing. UC Marin advises against waiting, because large leaves turn bitter and the plant may bolt.

For a steady supply, start a second box two or three weeks after the first; both Vermont and UC Marin give that interval. When a plant sends up a thick central stalk, it has bolted. Cut the whole thing and eat it, then let the next box take over.

## What goes wrong with spinach indoors?

The common indoor spinach problems are bolting from a warm room or a long lamp timer, stretching from low light, and fungus gnats from mix kept too wet. The fix for tall, pale seedlings is in the series post on [leggy seedlings](/blog/leggy-seedlings-indoors/). Fungus gnats usually fade once the top inch of mix dries between waterings.

If you later get a balcony, check your [lease and building rules](/blog/balcony-garden-lease-and-building-rules/) and the weight limit, then direct sow Bloomsdale 4 to 6 weeks before the last spring frost. The hand-built post on [vegetables for a balcony](/blog/best-vegetables-to-grow-on-a-balcony/) covers sun hours and pot sizes. Confirm that date with your county extension office; the [state planting calendars](/planting/) give the frost window.
