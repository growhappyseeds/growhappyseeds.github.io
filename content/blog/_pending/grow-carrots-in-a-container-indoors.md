This guide is for the apartment with no balcony: a bright window, a radiator somewhere near it, and a floor you are willing to give up a square foot of. The constraint is depth as much as light, because a carrot is a root, and a root stops where the pot does.

Danvers 126, the carrot in the Happy Seeds kit, is the worked example. Illinois Extension describes the Danvers type as thick and conical, up to 7 inches long, which sets the pot depth for everything below.

## Can you grow carrots in a pot indoors?

Yes, carrots grow in a pot indoors, in a container at least 12 inches deep, in the brightest window you have, with a lamp in winter. The University of Saskatchewan's [carrot guide](https://gardening.usask.ca/gardening-advice/gardenline-nested-pages/food-plant-pages/vegetables/carrots.php) calls indoor carrots possible but not very practical without supplemental lighting. That is the honest frame: it works, it is slow, and the crop is modest.

The series post on [five vegetables for a windowsill](/blog/windowsill-vegetables-from-seed/) ranks carrots last for a reason. Kale and spinach give baby leaves in about a month; carrots give roots after ten weeks or more.

## How deep does a carrot pot need to be?

A carrot pot needs to be at least 12 inches deep for Danvers 126, because the root grows straight down and needs loose mix for its whole length. Illinois Extension's guide to [growing carrots](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2020-04-20-how-grow-carrots) puts Danvers at up to 7 inches long and says the soil may need loosening 8 or 9 inches deep.

University of Maryland Extension's post on [carrots in containers](https://marylandgrows.umd.edu/2012/03/24/growing-carrots-in-containers/) gives the rule: soil depth of at least 150 percent of the carrot's length. For a 7-inch carrot that is 10.5 inches, and the kit's own container note says 12 inches. Saskatchewan's guide suggests a 5-gallon pail with drainage holes as the ideal container.

| Container | Depth | What you get from Danvers 126 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window box or tray | 4 to 6 inches | Carrot greens and stubby roots |
| Standard 8-inch pot | 8 inches | Short, thick roots that stop at the bottom |
| Tall pot or 5-gallon bucket | 12 inches or more | Full-length straight carrots |

Fill it with loose potting mix, not garden soil. Illinois Extension says rocks, clods and debris cause forked and twisted roots; fresh mix has none of those, so crowding and the pot bottom are the risks left.

## Where does a 12-inch pot go in a small apartment?

A 12-inch pot goes on the floor directly under the window, or on a low plant stand beside it, not on the sill. The sill is too narrow, and a deep pot of wet mix is heavy.

Keep the pot off the radiator cover, because the warm, dry air over it dries the seed surface quickly and carrots are a cool-season crop. The series post on [radiators and indoor seedlings](/blog/radiator-heat-and-indoor-seedlings/) shows how to find the cool, bright spot beside the heat. Put a deep saucer under the pot, and a tray under that if you rent.

## How much light do indoor carrots need?

Indoor carrots need a south or west window with several hours of direct sun, plus a lamp on a timer for 14 to 16 hours a day in winter. Saskatchewan's guide says carrots want full to partial sun and are not suited to shade. Vermont Extension's guide to [growing greens in winter](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/news/growing-greens-winter) puts the pot right in front of a south or west window; the 14-to-16-hour lamp figure is Iowa State and Minnesota Extension's seedling advice.

University of Minnesota Extension's guide to [growing carrots](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-carrots-and-parsnips) says green tops with little root usually mean crowding; weak light gives the same result. The series posts on [which window direction is best](/blog/best-window-direction-for-growing-vegetables/) and [grow lights for apartment seedlings](/blog/grow-lights-for-apartment-seedlings/) cover judging your window and a basic lamp setup.

## How do you sow carrot seeds in a pot?

Sow carrot seeds a quarter inch deep, about an inch apart across the whole surface, and keep that surface consistently moist until they sprout. Illinois Extension gives a quarter to a half inch, and Maryland's post says carrots do not need rows in a container. Sow them straight into the final pot, because Minnesota Extension says transplanting damages the taproot.

1. Fill the pot to an inch below the rim with potting mix and water until it drains from the bottom.
2. Scatter seeds about an inch apart, press them into the surface and cover with a quarter inch of mix.
3. Check the surface every day and mist or gently water when it starts to dry; Saskatchewan's guide says seed that dries out sprouts poorly.
4. Keep the pot in the brightest spot you have, at ordinary room temperature, 65 to 70 degrees F.
5. When tops are about 2 inches tall, thin to 2 inches apart by snipping, not pulling, and eat the thinnings.

Germination is slow and uneven, so do not give up early. Illinois Extension says carrots often take up to three weeks to sprout. Saskatchewan's guide puts it near 7 days at 68 degrees F and up to 17 days at 50 degrees F, so a cold floor runs slow. If nothing shows by week four, the series post on [why seeds do not germinate](/blog/why-are-my-seeds-not-germinating/) covers the usual causes.

## How do you water and feed indoor carrots?

Water when the top inch of mix is dry and feed lightly or not at all. Maryland Extension's rule for container carrots is moist but not soggy, and Minnesota Extension says drought makes roots bitter, tough and misshapen. The hand-built post on [vegetables for a balcony](/blog/best-vegetables-to-grow-on-a-balcony/) covers watering pots in general.

Minnesota Extension says excess nitrogen favors leaves over roots, so skip high-nitrogen feeds. A half-strength liquid vegetable feed about once a month is the usual container practice.

| Week | What to expect | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | Nothing, then thin green threads | Keep the surface damp daily |
| 3 to 5 | Ferny tops 2 inches tall | Thin to 2 inches apart with scissors |
| 5 to 9 | Tops thicken, orange shoulders may show | Cover any exposed shoulders with mix |
| 10 to 12 | Roots at least a half inch across | Pull one to check, then harvest as needed |

## When do you harvest carrots grown indoors?

Harvest indoor carrots when a test root is at least a half inch across at the shoulder, often 10 to 12 weeks after sowing and longer in a dim window. Illinois Extension gives that half-inch figure. Danvers 126 is listed at 65 to 75 days in a garden, and a window pot runs slower, so treat the packet's number as the earliest you should check.

Water the pot the day before, loosen the mix around one carrot and pull it straight up; Illinois Extension says the tops often break otherwise. If it is small, leave the rest another two weeks. Minnesota Extension says carrots can be harvested at any usable size, and Illinois warns that heavy watering near harvest can crack the roots.

## What goes wrong with carrots in a pot?

The common indoor carrot problems are seed that dried out before sprouting, a pot too shallow for the variety, crowding, and too little light. The first shows as an empty pot at week four; the others show as a forest of tops over stubby, twisted roots. Saskatchewan's guide blames crooked carrots on compacted soil and seedlings that were never thinned.

A second pot sown two or three weeks after the first, the interval Minnesota Extension suggests, keeps carrots coming. If you later get a balcony, check your [lease and building rules](/blog/balcony-garden-lease-and-building-rules/) and the weight limit, then direct sow Danvers 126 two to three weeks before the last spring frost. Confirm that date with your county extension office; the [state planting calendars](/planting/) give the frost window.
