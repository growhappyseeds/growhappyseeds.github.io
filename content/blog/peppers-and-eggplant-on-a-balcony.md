This guide is for the apartment with a balcony, terrace or small patio that gets real afternoon sun. Peppers and eggplant ask the most of a city balcony: six or more hours of direct light, warm nights, a big pot each and a long wait. The constraints are sun, heat off the walls, pot weight and the lease.

Before anything else, read your lease and the building's rules on balcony planters, covered in the series post on [rental balcony garden rules](/blog/balcony-garden-lease-and-building-rules/). Then check the weight, because two 5-gallon pots of wet mix can weigh 80 pounds or more together; the [balcony weight limits post](/blog/balcony-weight-limits-for-containers/) shows how to find your limit. A fire escape is never the answer, because it is an exit and blocking it breaks fire code in most cities.

## Can peppers and eggplant grow on a city balcony?

Peppers and eggplant grow on a city balcony that gets at least six hours of direct sun, and more is better. [Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-peppers-home-garden) puts six hours as the minimum for peppers and says they prefer 8 to 10, and [Georgia Extension](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/C1028/home-garden-eggplant/) asks for 8 to 10 hours for eggplant. A south, southwest or west balcony usually gets there in summer; an east balcony is often borderline for peppers and rarely enough for eggplant, so count the hours first.

The sun test is the same one used for tomatoes. The site's guide to [the best vegetables for a balcony](/blog/best-vegetables-to-grow-on-a-balcony/) explains how to count your hours and what a shadier balcony can grow instead. The series post on [tomatoes on a small balcony](/blog/tomatoes-on-a-small-balcony/) is the sister guide to this one, and the same sunny corner serves all three.

Both crops stay compact in a pot, roughly 2 to 3 feet tall, so one of each fits a balcony only a few feet deep.

## What pot do peppers and eggplant need on a balcony?

Each plant needs its own pot of 5 gallons or more, one pepper or one eggplant per pot. Maryland Extension gives 5 gallons as the minimum for a pepper, and [North Carolina A&T's container guide](https://ncat.edu/caes/cooperative-extension/covid-19/fruiting-vegetables.php) gives the same floor for tomato, pepper and eggplant. The Happy Seeds kit lists 3 to 5 gallons for its Anaheim Chili pepper and 5 gallons for its Black Beauty eggplant.

Two details are specific to these crops. North Carolina A&T prefers light-colored plastic, because a dark pot in full balcony sun cooks the roots of a heat-loving plant. It also says to set a short stake or a small two-ring cage at planting; pepper branches are brittle, and one eggplant can carry several pounds of fruit.

Pot materials, potting mix, saucers and drips are the same for every balcony crop and are covered in [the best vegetables for a balcony](/blog/best-vegetables-to-grow-on-a-balcony/).

## When do you start pepper and eggplant seeds for a balcony?

Start both indoors about 8 weeks before your last spring frost and move the plants out about 2 weeks after it. Those are the kit's own timings for Anaheim Chili and Black Beauty, inside Maryland Extension's 8 to 10 weeks for peppers and Georgia's 6 to 8 weeks for eggplant. Find your window on the [state planting calendars](/planting/), read [when to start seeds indoors](/blog/when-to-start-seeds-indoors/), and confirm the date with your county extension office.

Both seeds want warmth to germinate. [Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers-home-gardens) calls 70 degrees F ideal for peppers, and [Utah State Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/eggplant-in-the-garden.php) germinates eggplant at 80 degrees. The series post on [where to start seeds in a small apartment](/blog/starting-seeds-in-a-small-apartment/) covers the warm spots a heated apartment already has.

Do not rush the move outdoors. Minnesota Extension says to transplant peppers only after nighttime lows stay above 50 degrees F, and Georgia wants the soil at 65 to 70 before eggplant goes out. A plant set out into a cold week on an exposed balcony stalls for longer than it would have waited by the window.

| Step | Pepper (Anaheim Chili) | Eggplant (Black Beauty) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sow indoors | 8 weeks before last frost | 8 weeks before last frost |
| Germination warmth | About 70 degrees F soil (Minnesota Extension) | About 80 degrees F, then 65 to 75 (Utah State) |
| Move to balcony | 2 weeks after last frost, nights above 50 F | 2 weeks after last frost, soil 65 to 70 F |
| Pot | 3 to 5 gallons | 5 gallons |
| First harvest | 75 to 80 days from transplant | 75 to 85 days from transplant |

Harden the seedlings off over a week or two before the move, following the series post on [moving apartment seedlings outside](/blog/start-indoors-move-to-balcony/).

## How do you water and feed them in pots?

Water whenever the top 2 inches of mix are dry, which in July usually means every morning, and feed weekly once the plants are established. The daily check is North Carolina A&T's rule for container eggplant. Utah State says irregular watering gives eggplant tough, leathery fruit, and Maryland says dry spells make peppers drop blossoms.

Feeding is where these two differ from windowsill greens. North Carolina A&T feeds container peppers and eggplant weekly with a balanced soluble fertilizer from about two weeks after planting, then a high-potassium feed at flowering. Minnesota Extension adds a warning for peppers: too much nitrogen gives a bushy, leafy plant that is slow to set fruit.

## Why do balcony pepper flowers drop, and what about eggplant?

Pepper flowers drop on a balcony mostly because of temperature, eggplant suffers in the same heat, and the fix for both is afternoon shade and steady water. [Iowa State Extension](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-peppers-home-garden) says pepper flowers may abort when nights fall below 60 degrees F or days rise above 85, with the best fruit set between 70 and 80. A south or west balcony against brick runs well above the forecast on a July afternoon.

During a heat wave, rig shade cloth or an old sheet on the sunny side for the afternoon, keep the pots watered, and wait for cooler nights. The extension guides give no flower-drop threshold for eggplant. Utah State notes that nights below 55 or days above 95 give tough-skinned fruit, so the same shade helps it too.

Pollination is rarely the problem outdoors, because both crops self-pollinate when the plant moves in the breeze. If flowers open and fall in mild, calm weather, tap the stake gently around midday a few times a week.

| Symptom | Likely cause on a balcony | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pepper flowers drop in a hot week | Days above 85 F, or nights below 60 F (Iowa State) | Afternoon shade, water daily, wait for milder nights |
| Flowers drop after the mix went bone dry | Drought stress | Water deeper and more often; add a reservoir saucer |
| Tiny holes in eggplant leaves in spring | Flea beetles | Cover young plants with a floating row cover; established plants tolerate it |
| Eggplant fruit dull and soft | Picked too late | Harvest at glossy and firm; check every two days in August |
| Leafy pepper, few flowers | Too much nitrogen or too little sun | Switch to a high-potassium feed; move the pot to the sunniest spot |

Flea beetles are the one pest worth watching, and they reach balconies too. Georgia Extension describes pinhead-sized holes in young eggplant leaves. A floating row cover over the seedlings for the first few weeks keeps the beetles off, and an established plant shrugs them off.

## How long until you pick a balcony pepper or eggplant?

Expect the first Anaheim peppers 75 to 80 days after the plants go out, and the first Black Beauty eggplant 75 to 85 days after, the kit's figures from transplant. A move in late May puts the first harvest in mid-August, and both plants keep producing until frost. Patience is the main input here.

Pick peppers green or let them ripen red. Minnesota Extension says you do not need to wait for peppers to turn red, and Maryland adds that ripe fruit is sweeter but does not ripen further once picked. Cut the stem with scissors rather than pulling, because a pulled pepper takes a branch with it.

Harvest eggplant when the skin is glossy and the fruit feels firm when squeezed, Georgia Extension's test. Utah State adds that overripe fruit turns dull, soft and bitter, so check every couple of days once the fruit sizes up. Before the first fall frost, pull the whole pepper plant and hang it indoors; Maryland Extension says the last fruit ripens that way.
