Maine planting calendar

When to plant vegetables in Maine

Updated 2026-11-01 · USDA zones 3b to 6b

In Maine the last spring frost usually falls mid-May (coast) to early June (north) and the first fall frost mid-September to early October (USDA zones 3b to 6b). Sow cool-season crops like spinach, kale, carrots, beets and turnips 2 to 6 weeks before the last frost and again in late summer for fall; move tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, cucumbers and melons outside 1 to 2 weeks after the last frost.

The Maine fall garden

The cool-season five (kale, spinach, carrot, beet, turnip) taste sweeter after a light frost. Counting back from a first frost mid-September to early October, sow spinach 6 to 8 weeks earlier and the other four 8 to 12 weeks earlier. In the warmest parts of the state the fall sowing is often the better one.

Where these dates come from

Zone ranges follow the 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map. Frost windows are typical for the populated parts of Maine; your town can differ by two weeks or more. The authoritative local source is the Maine cooperative extension service.

Common questions

When is the last frost in Maine?

Typically mid-May (coast) to early June (north), depending on where in the state you are and your elevation. Confirm your own date with Maine's extension service before you plant.

What vegetables can I plant first in Maine?

Spinach, kale, carrots, beets and turnips go in first; they tolerate cool soil and light frost. Start tomatoes, peppers and eggplant indoors and wait until after the last frost to transplant.

Can I grow a fall garden in Maine?

Yes. Count back from the first fall frost (mid-September to early October): sow spinach 6 to 8 weeks before it, and kale, carrots, beets and turnips 8 to 12 weeks before it.

What USDA hardiness zone is Maine?

Maine spans roughly zones 3b to 6b on the 2023 USDA map. Zones describe winter lows, which matter for perennials; for annual vegetables your frost dates matter more.
