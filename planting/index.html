Planting calendars

When to plant vegetables, state by state.

Frost windows, USDA zones and a sowing calendar for the ten vegetables in the Happy Seeds kit, for every US state.

How to use these calendars

Every planting date comes from two numbers: your area's average last spring frost and first fall frost. Each state page gives the typical window for both, then counts forward and back from them for ten common vegetables.

Cool-season crops (spinach, kale, carrots, beets, turnips) go in the ground 2 to 6 weeks before the last frost and again in late summer for a fall harvest. Warm-season crops (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, cucumbers, melons) wait until the soil is warm, 1 to 2 weeks after the last frost; tomatoes, peppers and eggplant are usually started indoors 6 to 8 weeks earlier.

Frost dates vary by elevation and by how close you are to water, so treat the state window as a starting point and confirm the date for your town with your county extension office, linked on each page.
