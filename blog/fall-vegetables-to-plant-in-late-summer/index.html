Fall sowing

Fall vegetables to plant in late summer: the cool-season five.

By Happy Seeds · November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

For gardeners who want a second harvest from the same bed or balcony, with a frost count-back table and five crops that do not mind the cold.

The short answerKale, spinach, carrots, beets and turnips are the fall vegetables to plant in late summer. Count back from your average first frost date: sow spinach and turnips 6 to 8 weeks before it, kale and beets 8 to 10 weeks before, and carrots 10 to 12 weeks before. They grow best as the weather cools, and a light frost makes most of them sweeter.

Why do cool-season vegetables like late summer sowing?

Because they grow up as the weather cools down, and cool weather is what they want. Kale, spinach, carrots, beets and turnips all germinate happily in warm soil, but they make their best leaves and roots when days are shorter and nights drop into the 40s and 50s Fahrenheit. Sow them in late summer and the timing works in your favor: the seeds sprout fast in the heat, then the plants mature in the mild, damp weather of early fall.

The same crops sown in late spring often go wrong. Spinach bolts (sends up a flower stalk and turns bitter) the moment June heat arrives. Turnips get woody. A fall sowing avoids all of that, because the plants never meet a hot stretch at the wrong moment. Many leaf-eating pests also wind down by September, so a fall bed is usually cleaner than a spring one.

There is one more quiet benefit. The garden is already built. Your containers, your bed, your watering habit from summer: all of it carries straight into a second season without any setup.

When should I plant fall vegetables? Count back from first frost

Find your average first fall frost date, then count back the number of weeks each vegetable needs to mature, and sow on or before that date. Your local extension office or a quick search for "first frost date" plus your town will give you the average. In much of the northern United States that lands in late September or October; in the South it can be November or later.

The ranges below are typical for each vegetable type, not a promise for any particular packet. Add one or two extra weeks to the count, because growth slows as the days shorten. A crop that takes 50 days in June may take 60 in September.

Vegetable typeTypical days to maturitySow this many weeks before first frost
Spinach35 to 45 days6 to 8 weeks
Turnip40 to 55 days7 to 9 weeks
Kale50 to 65 days (baby leaves sooner)8 to 10 weeks
Beet50 to 65 days8 to 10 weeks
Carrot65 to 80 days10 to 12 weeks

So if your first frost is around October 15, carrots go in by late July, beets and kale by early to mid August, and spinach and turnips can wait until late August or the first days of September. If your frost is November 15, slide everything a month later. That is why "late summer" means something different in Minnesota than it does in Georgia.

Which vegetables should a beginner plant for fall?

Start with five: kale, spinach, carrots, beets and turnips. They are the classic cool-season crops, they all sow directly in the ground or a container (no transplanting), and they cover the two things fall does best, leaves and roots. These are also the five fall packets inside the Veggie Garden Kit, and the 24-page guide gives each one its own depth, spacing and watering notes.

  1. Kale 'Red Russian'. Flat, blue-green leaves with purple stems, tender enough to eat raw when young. Pick outer leaves as you go and the plant keeps producing. If you only have room for one fall crop, this is the one.
  2. Spinach 'Bloomsdale'. A crinkled, dark-leaved spinach that grows fast and tastes better after a cold night. Sow a short row every two weeks for a steady supply instead of one big flush.
  3. Turnip 'Purple Top'. The fastest root in the group. Harvest at two or three inches across for the mildest flavor, and eat the greens too.
  4. Beet 'Chioggia'. Red and white rings inside when you slice it. Each beet "seed" is a cluster that sprouts several seedlings, so thin them to about three inches apart. The thinnings are salad.
  5. Carrot 'Danvers 126'. A sturdy, tapered carrot that copes with heavier soil better than the long, skinny types. Carrots are slow. That's normal. Seeds can take two to three weeks to show, and the roots need the longest run to frost of anything here, so sow them first.

How much frost can fall vegetables take?

All five handle a light frost (around 28 to 32 degrees Fahrenheit), and several of them taste better for it. Cold triggers the plants to store more sugar in their leaves and roots, which is why a kale leaf picked after frost is sweeter than one picked in August.

  • Kale and spinach shrug off light frost. Kale in particular keeps going well into cold weather, and in milder climates it stands through winter.
  • Carrots sweeten noticeably once the soil chills. The tops will wilt in a hard freeze, but the roots are fine underground for weeks. A layer of straw or shredded leaves over the row keeps the ground soft enough to dig.
  • Beets and turnips take light frost but not a hard freeze. Pull them once nights settle in the mid-20s.

A hard freeze (the mid-20s and below) is the real end of the season for leaves above ground. When it is in the forecast, pick what is ready and cover the rest with an old sheet overnight if you want a few more days.

How do I sow fall seeds when it is still hot out?

Keep the seedbed shaded and evenly moist until the seedlings are up. The hard part of a fall garden is not fall; it is August. The soil is hot and dries fast, and spinach in particular germinates poorly when the ground is above about 75 degrees Fahrenheit. Here is what works.

  1. Water the bed or container deeply the evening before you sow, so the soil is cool and damp at the start.
  2. Sow in the evening, a touch deeper than you would in spring (the packet depth plus a quarter inch is plenty). Deeper soil is cooler and holds moisture longer.
  3. Shade the row. A piece of shade cloth, a board propped on bricks, or an old window screen laid over the bed all work. Remove it as soon as you see sprouts.
  4. Water lightly every day until germination. A seed that dries out mid-sprout is finished, and this is the single most common reason a late-summer sowing fails.
  5. Once the plants have a few true leaves, water deeply but less often, and mulch around them to keep roots cool.

For spinach, you can also chill the seed packet in the refrigerator for a week before sowing. It is an old trick and it helps more often than not.

Can I grow a fall garden in containers on a balcony?

Yes, and fall is arguably the best season to try it. All five of these crops suit a planter or a sunny windowsill, the weather is kinder to small pots than July is, and you will not be fighting the heat that bakes balcony containers in midsummer.

Use the deepest container you can for roots: 10 to 12 inches for carrots and beets, 8 inches for turnips. Kale and spinach are happy in 6 to 8 inches of soil. A standard window box grows a surprising amount of spinach. Put the planter where it gets the most direct sun you have, because as the sun drops lower in the sky through September, a spot that got six hours in July may get four. Containers also dry faster than ground beds, so keep checking them even when the air feels cool. For pot sizes and sun placement in more detail, see the best vegetables to grow on a balcony.

What if I missed the late-summer window?

Sow the same five crops in early spring instead. Kale, spinach, carrots, beets and turnips are two-season vegetables, and a spring sowing as soon as the ground can be worked gives you an early harvest before the heat arrives. In mild-winter regions you can often sow spinach and kale right through fall for a winter crop.

Common questions

Is September too late to plant vegetables?

Not for the fast ones. Spinach, turnips and baby kale can go in up to about six weeks before your first frost, which for many gardens means early to mid September. Carrots and beets need a longer run and are better sown in August.

How do I find my first frost date?

Search for the average first frost date for your town or ZIP code, or ask your local cooperative extension office. It is an average, not a guarantee, so give yourself a week or two of margin.

Can I leave carrots in the ground over winter?

In many climates, yes. Carrots hold well in cold soil and get sweeter, so cover the row with several inches of straw or leaves and dig them as you need them until the ground freezes solid.

Why won't my spinach seeds germinate in August?

Spinach germinates poorly when the soil is above about 75 degrees Fahrenheit. Sow in the evening into soil you watered the night before, shade the row until sprouts show, and keep it moist every day. Chilling the packet in the fridge for a week also helps.

What is the easiest fall vegetable for a beginner?

Kale, and Kale 'Red Russian' in particular. It germinates easily, handles frost, and you harvest a few outer leaves at a time so one plant feeds you for months.
