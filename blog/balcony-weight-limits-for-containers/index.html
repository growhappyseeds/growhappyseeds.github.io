How Much Does a Wet Planter Weigh? Balcony Weight Limits

By Happy Seeds · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

What a wet pot really weighs, what the building code assumes your balcony carries, where the heavy pots should sit, and how to grow the same vegetables with less weight.

The short answer A 5-gallon pot of wet potting mix weighs roughly 30 to 45 pounds, and a 20-inch pot can reach 100 pounds. US building codes commonly design residential balconies for 40 pounds per square foot spread evenly, so keep heavy pots against the building wall, use soilless mix, and ask your building before adding large planters.

This guide is for the apartment gardener with a balcony, terrace or roof deck, where the limit is not light but weight. A balcony is a slab or a deck hanging off the side of a building, and a vegetable garden in big pots is heavy, wet and permanent.

Here is what a wet planter actually weighs, what the code assumes the balcony carries, where heavy pots should sit, and how to cut the weight. For sun hours, wind and pot sizes by crop, start with the best vegetables to grow on a balcony.

How much does a wet planter weigh?

A wet planter weighs several times what the empty pot and the bag of mix suggest, because water is the heavy part. The University of Maryland Extension's guide to growing vegetables in containers says a 20-inch container filled with moist potting mix and plants can weigh 100 pounds. A gallon of water weighs about 8.3 pounds, and a thorough watering leaves a lot of it in the mix.

The table gives rough working figures for a peat-based mix watered until it drains, plus a plastic pot. They are estimates, not measurements of your pots, so weigh one container on a bathroom scale after watering.

Container Mix it holds Rough weight, wet
Small pot, about 2 gallons 0.3 cubic feet 15 to 25 pounds
5-gallon bucket or pot 0.7 cubic feet 30 to 45 pounds
10-gallon tub 1.3 cubic feet 60 to 90 pounds
20-inch pot, about 15 gallons 2 cubic feet about 100 pounds

Terracotta, ceramic and concrete pots add their own weight on top, and a mix with garden soil in it weighs far more. Maryland Extension puts soil at about 75 pounds per cubic foot against about 10 for dry soilless media. Four warm-season vegetables in 5-gallon pots plus one melon tub weigh roughly as much as two adults, standing there all summer.

How much weight can a balcony hold?

US building codes commonly design a residential balcony for 40 pounds per square foot, spread evenly over the whole surface. The current International Building Code table sets exterior balconies and decks at the same live load as the occupancy they serve. For private rooms in multifamily buildings that is 40 pounds per square foot, as in Massachusetts' copy of Table 1607.1.

The same table designs a roof used as a roof garden for 100 pounds per square foot, while an ordinary roof is not designed for people and pots. A 4 by 8 foot balcony at 40 pounds per square foot is designed for about 1,280 pounds spread across all 32 square feet. That total includes you, your guests and the furniture, not just the garden.

Treat the code figure as a design minimum, not as your balcony's real capacity. Older code editions asked more of balconies, and local amendments, the building's age, water damage and the way the balcony is supported all change the answer. The building's drawings say what was designed, and only a structural engineer can confirm what a particular balcony will carry today.

Where should heavy pots sit on a balcony?

Heavy pots belong against the wall where the balcony meets the building, spread out rather than clustered. The Royal Horticultural Society's advice on roof gardens and balconies says heavy containers should be placed near load-bearing walls or over a load-bearing beam or joist. On a cantilevered balcony, the kind with no posts underneath, the building wall is the supported edge and the outer railing is the free end.

The horticulturist Stephen Albert makes the same point in his balcony and rooftop garden basics: balconies are often suspended from the building and cannot bear heavy weight. On a balcony carried by posts, the corners beside the posts are the strong spots. The middle of the span and a cluster of big tubs at the outer edge are the places to avoid.

Keep pots off the railing unless the building allows it and the bracket is rated for a full, wet box, because a planter over a sidewalk is a falling hazard. Never put pots on a fire escape, because it is an emergency exit and blocking it breaks fire code in most cities.

How do you lighten a balcony container garden?

Switch to a soilless mix, lighter pots and the smallest container each crop actually needs. The University of Wisconsin Extension's container gardening guide recommends soilless mixes where many containers sit on a balcony or similar structure and excess weight can be a problem. Peat or coir with perlite or vermiculite is light when dry and still lighter than soil when wet.

Fabric grow bags, plastic and resin pots weigh a fraction of terracotta or concrete, and Maryland Extension notes that fabric bags are lightweight and portable. Skip the layer of gravel in the bottom of the pot, because it adds weight without improving drainage. Empty saucers after watering so you are not storing gallons of water on the slab.

Then size each pot to the crop instead of buying the biggest tub on the shelf. Maryland Extension's minimum volumes run from half a gallon for beets to 5 gallons for standard tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers and eggplant. Bigger pots hold more water and crop more reliably, but every gallon of mix above the minimum is weight the balcony carries all season.

What do the lease and the building rules say about planters?

Read the lease, the house rules or the condo bylaws before buying a single pot, because many already limit what a balcony may hold. Common rules cover planters on or over the railing, drip onto the balcony below, and a weight limit that management will quote if you ask. A note to the super that you are adding large containers costs nothing and starts the conversation about the load limit.

Neighbors matter as much as rules. Water that runs out of a 10-gallon tub lands on the balcony below, so keep a tray under anything large. A trellis or cage adds little weight but catches wind, which the balcony post covers.

Common questions

How much weight can an apartment balcony hold?

Current building codes based on the International Building Code design exterior balconies for the same live load as the rooms they serve, 40 pounds per square foot for private areas of multifamily buildings. That is a design minimum for evenly spread weight, not a promise about your balcony. Only the building's drawings or a structural engineer can say more.

How much does a 5-gallon pot of soil weigh when wet?

Roughly 30 to 45 pounds with a peat-based potting mix watered until it drains, plus the weight of the pot. A mix that contains garden soil weighs a good deal more. Weigh one pot on a bathroom scale after a thorough watering to get a number for your own containers.

Is it safe to put large planters on a balcony?

Usually yes, if you keep the heaviest ones against the building wall, spread them out rather than clustering them, and stay well inside the balcony's total design load. Ask the building manager or super before adding anything large, because many leases and building rules limit planters, especially on the railing.

Where is the strongest part of a balcony for heavy pots?

Against the wall where the balcony meets the building, which is the supported edge of a cantilevered balcony, or beside the posts of a post-supported one. The Royal Horticultural Society says heavy containers belong near load-bearing walls or over a beam or joist. The outer edge and the middle of the span are the weakest places.

What is the lightest potting mix for a balcony?

A soilless mix of peat or coir with perlite or vermiculite. University of Maryland Extension puts soilless media at about 10 pounds per cubic foot dry, against about 75 for soil. University of Wisconsin Extension recommends soilless mixes when many containers sit on a balcony where excess weight can be a problem.

Do I need to tell my landlord about a balcony garden?

Read the lease first; many leases and condo rules already say what the balcony may hold and whether anything may hang from the railing. Telling the super or management before you add large containers protects you if a neighbor complains about drips or a pot in the wind, and it is the only way to learn the balcony's load limit.
