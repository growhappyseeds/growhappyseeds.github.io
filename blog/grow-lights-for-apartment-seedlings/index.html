Do You Need a Grow Light to Start Seeds in an Apartment?

By Happy Seeds · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

When a window is enough, when it is not, and what a basic light setup looks like in a small apartment.

The short answer You need a grow light to start seeds in an apartment unless you have an unobstructed south-facing window and are only growing quick greens. Tomatoes, peppers and eggplant spend six to eight weeks indoors and turn tall and floppy at a window. Give them a cheap LED shop light on a timer, 14 to 16 hours a day.

This guide is for the apartment gardener with no balcony, and for the balcony gardener who starts seeds inside before the weather allows them out. In both apartments the question is the same: is the window enough, or do you need a lamp?

Here is how to tell, and what a simple setup looks like when the answer is a lamp. For what each window direction can grow on its own, read which window direction is best for growing vegetables indoors.

Do you need a grow light to start seeds in an apartment?

You need a grow light to start seeds in an apartment unless you have an unobstructed south-facing window and are growing only quick cool-season greens. University of Minnesota Extension's guide to lighting for indoor plants and starting seeds says a high-light spot may start seeds without artificial lighting. It adds that seeds that need more time indoors, such as tomatoes and peppers, may become leggy without extra light.

That is the whole answer in one sentence. Time indoors is what decides it, not the crop's size or its sun needs later on. Kale sown in a sill pot at a south window grows fine; a tomato seedling at the same window for seven weeks is a stretched, pale thing that falls over.

A North Dakota State University Extension agent describes the usual result in Grow lights for seed starting. After many tries at starting seeds in south-facing windows, her seedlings were tall, thin and floppy, and she switched to lights. A city window with a building across the street gets even fewer hours of direct sun.

What a basic grow light setup looks like in a small apartment

A basic setup is one LED shop light, two short chains or a shelf to hang it from, a plug-in timer and a tray that fits underneath. University of New Hampshire Extension's fact sheet on growing seedlings under lights says most seedlings can be grown to transplant stage under four-bulb T8 fluorescent shop lights. A bright white LED shop light of similar length generally does the same job, runs cooler and weighs less, which matters on a rental shelf.

Hang the light from a bookshelf, a wire kitchen rack or a closet rod with the chains that come with the fixture. Put the tray on the shelf below and adjust the chain, not the tray, as the seedlings grow. Books under the tray work when the fixture cannot move.

University of Vermont Extension's tips for choosing grow lights say a bulb rated near 6500K is ideal, because that matches natural daylight. The same page suggests a color rendering index above 85, and says seedlings need 16 to 18 hours of light a day at 65 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit. Color temperature is printed on most shop-light boxes and CRI on many, and most hardware stores carry a fixture that meets them.

Skip the dim purple lights sold as seedling kits. The UNH fact sheet measured one cheap purple LED that would have needed 34 hours of running time a day to give seedlings enough light, which is impossible. Incandescent bulbs are worse: NDSU Extension says they do not work well for starting seeds and generate a lot of heat.

How far above the seedlings, and how many hours a day?

Keep fluorescent tubes a few inches above the seedlings, LEDs 8 to 12 inches above, and run the light 14 to 16 hours a day on a timer. Iowa State University Extension's answer to how do I set up supplemental lights for my seedlings gives the distances. It says 4 to 6 inches for most fluorescent fixtures and about 8 to 12 inches for LEDs.

The same page sets the timer at 14 to 16 hours a day. University of Minnesota Extension says 16 to 18 hours for seedlings, so anywhere in that range is fine.

The timer is the part people skip, and the part that makes the system work in an apartment. Without it, the light goes on when you remember and off at bedtime, so the seedlings get a short, uneven day that changes with your schedule. A mechanical plug-in timer from the hardware store solves it.

Watch the seedlings, not the ruler. If they lean and stretch, the light is too high or too weak; if the newest leaves look washed out, it is too close.

Iowa State also recommends air circulation around the seedlings to carry off the fixture's heat. A small fan on low does that, and gentle air movement is widely credited with sturdier stems.

Timing: when the lamp goes on, and when it goes off for the year

The lamp goes on the day the first seedlings break the surface, and it goes off for the year once the last transplant has moved outside. Most vegetable seeds do not need light to sprout, so the tray can sit in a warm spot until then. From the first green loop of stem onward, every hour without strong light is an hour of stretching.

Count backward from your last spring frost to set the start date, and confirm that date with your county extension office before you sow. The planting guide lists frost windows by state, and when to start seeds indoors covers the crop-by-crop schedule.

Where the last frost comes in late April or May, one light running from late February to mid May covers the tomato, pepper and eggplant packets. Shift that window to match your own frost date.

Common questions

Can I start seeds in a south-facing window without a grow light?

Sometimes: University of Minnesota Extension says an unobstructed south window may be enough to start seeds, but tomatoes and peppers, which need more time indoors, may become leggy without extra light. Greens sown in a pot that stays at the window usually manage. Slow warm-season crops usually do not.

How many hours a day should a grow light be on for seedlings?

Fourteen to sixteen hours a day on a timer is the usual advice. Iowa State University Extension says 14 to 16 hours, and University of Minnesota Extension says 16 to 18. University of Vermont Extension notes that plants need periods of darkness, so never leave it on around the clock.

How far above seedlings should a grow light hang?

Close: Iowa State University Extension puts most fluorescent fixtures 4 to 6 inches above the seedlings and LED fixtures a little higher, at about 8 to 12 inches. Raise the fixture as the plants grow. If the seedlings stretch toward the light, it is too far away.

Will a regular LED shop light work as a grow light?

Usually, yes, for seedlings: University of New Hampshire Extension says most seedlings can be grown to transplant stage under four-bulb T8 fluorescent shop lights. A bright white LED shop light rated near 6500K generally does the same job in a smaller, cooler fixture. Skip incandescent bulbs, which run hot and do little for plants.

Do seeds need light to germinate?

Most vegetable seeds in a beginner kit do not need light to sprout; they need warmth and moisture. Light matters from the moment the seedling breaks the surface. Check the tray daily and move it under the lamp as soon as the first stems appear, because a day of stretching in the dark is hard to undo.

Does a grow light raise the electric bill much?

A single LED shop light is one of the cheaper things in the apartment to run. The exact cost depends on your fixture's wattage, your hours and your utility rate, so check the label and your bill rather than a rule of thumb. The light runs for about ten weeks in late winter and spring, not all year.
