Leggy Seedlings Indoors: Why They Stretch and How to Fix Them

By Happy Seeds · November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

What makes apartment seedlings tall and floppy, which ones can be saved, and the light, heat and spacing changes that keep the next tray short.

The short answer Seedlings get leggy when they have too little light and too much warmth, so they stretch toward the window on thin stems. Fix it with brighter light a few inches above the plants for 12 to 16 hours a day, a cooler spot, and by burying leggy tomato stems deeper at transplant.

This guide is for anyone starting seeds inside an apartment, with or without a balcony. The constraint is light: a window gives a few hours of sideways sun, and a radiator under the sill adds heat the seedlings do not need. Together those two make the tall, pale, floppy seedlings that flop over within a few weeks.

The fix is mostly about light, then temperature, then spacing. Each section below starts with the short answer.

Why do seedlings get leggy indoors?

Seedlings get leggy indoors because they do not get enough light, so they spend their energy growing taller toward it instead of growing sturdy. University of Minnesota Extension's guide to starting seeds indoors calls lack of light the major cause of elongated, skinny stems. It describes windowsill seedlings as too tall, with thin, bent stems.

Warmth makes it worse. Purdue Extension's note on leggy tomatoes says warm temperatures aggravate spindly growth, and its advice is as much light as possible and a cooler spot. A heated city apartment in winter is the classic setup: weak window light and a warm room.

Crowding adds a third push. Seedlings packed into one pot shade each other and stretch to get above their neighbors.

How do you fix leggy seedlings?

You fix leggy seedlings by moving them into much stronger light right away, then keeping them cooler and less crowded. The stretch they already have will not shrink, but new growth will come in shorter and thicker. That is usually enough to get them to transplant size.

University of Minnesota Extension says to keep lights no more than 4 inches above the seedlings, with 2 inches as ideal for the shop lights its guide describes. It recommends 12 to 16 hours of light a day, with a dark period each night. Check your own fixture's directions, since some LEDs hang a little higher.

If you are using a window only, put the tray right against the glass and turn it every day. The post on grow lights for apartment seedlings covers when a window is enough and what a basic lamp setup looks like.

Can you bury leggy seedlings deeper?

You can bury leggy tomato seedlings deeper, but most other vegetables should stay at their original depth. Purdue Extension explains that tomatoes root along the stem, so a stretched plant can be set deeper or laid at an angle in a trench. It adds that other crops do not root as readily and should not be planted deeper.

For a tomato, strip the lower leaves that would end up underground, then pot it up into a deeper container. Leave the top few sets of leaves above the mix. Do the same again when it moves to its final container outdoors.

For peppers, eggplant and cucumbers, keep the soil line where it was. A short stake and a soft tie can hold a floppy stem upright while new, sturdier growth catches up. Hold seedlings by a leaf, never the stem, when you move them.

What about leggy greens on a windowsill?

Leggy greens are usually better resown than rescued, because they are quick and cheap to start again. Kale, spinach, beet greens and turnip greens that stretched in a dim window will rarely bulk up. A fresh sowing in better light catches up within a few weeks.

Before you resow, fix the cause. Move the pot to the brightest window you have, or add a lamp, and keep it off the radiator. The guide on which window direction is best for vegetables helps you judge your exposure.

Sow thinly so the seedlings are not fighting for light. The post on vegetables you can grow from seed on a windowsill gives pot depths and harvest times for each green.

How do you stop the next tray from getting leggy?

You stop the next tray from getting leggy by having strong light ready the day the first seedlings break the surface. Most of these seeds can sprout in a warm spot without light, but check them daily. Once the first green loop appears, every hour in weak light is an hour of stretching.

Thin early. University of Minnesota Extension recommends cutting all but the healthiest seedling at soil level with scissors, rather than pulling them. Pulling disturbs the roots of the one you keep.

Keep the room on the cool side once they are up, away from radiators and heat vents. A gentle breeze from a small fan helps stems firm up. A light feed at quarter strength once true leaves appear, as the Minnesota guide suggests, keeps them green without forcing soft growth.

Are you starting seeds too early?

Many leggy seedlings were simply started too early, so they ran out of room and light before they could go outside. Tomatoes want 6 to 8 weeks indoors, and peppers and eggplant about 8. Purdue's advice for leggy tomatoes includes starting a little later next year.

Count back from your last spring frost date and confirm that date with your county extension office. The state planting calendars give a starting window, and the post on when to start seeds indoors has the timing by crop.

Common questions

What causes leggy seedlings?

Too little light is the main cause, according to University of Minnesota Extension. The seedling stretches its stem toward the brightest spot it can find. Warm rooms make it worse, which is why seedlings on a sill above a radiator often stretch the fastest. Crowded seedlings also stretch as they compete for the same light.

Can leggy seedlings be saved?

Often, yes. Move them into stronger light right away so new growth stays compact. Tomatoes can be set deeper when you pot them up or transplant them, because they root along the buried stem. Most other crops do not, so give them more light and support instead, or sow a fresh batch if they are quick greens.

How far should a grow light be from seedlings?

Close. University of Minnesota Extension says to keep lights no more than 4 inches above the tops of the seedlings, For the fluorescent shop lights its guide describes, 2 inches is ideal. Raise the fixture as the plants grow. If seedlings keep leaning or stretching, the light is too far away or too weak.

Should you bury leggy seedlings deeper?

Only tomatoes, as a rule. Purdue Extension notes that tomatoes root along the stem, so a leggy plant can go in deeper or at an angle in a trench. It says other crops do not root as readily and should not be planted deeper. Keep peppers, eggplant and cucumbers at their original depth.

How many hours of light do seedlings need?

University of Minnesota Extension says seedlings need 12 to 16 hours of light a day, plus a dark period each night. A winter window rarely gives that many hours of strong light. A lamp on an outlet timer is the simplest way to keep the hours steady without remembering to switch it on.
